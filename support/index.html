Support blitcp

blitcp is free, Apache-2.0 licensed, and stays that way. There is no paid tier, no telemetry, and nothing about this page is ever printed by the tool itself. If it has saved you time and you would like to put something back, here is exactly where it goes.

Donate

Ko-fi Liberapay

Ko-fi takes one-off payments and cards; Liberapay is non-profit, takes no cut, and is built for small recurring amounts. Both reach the same person. There is also a Sponsor button on the repository.

What it pays for

Not a salary — three specific costs that the project currently absorbs.

  1. A Windows code signing certificate. The Windows binaries are unsigned, so the first launch shows “Windows protected your PC” and everyone has to click through a warning that says nothing about what the program does. Certificates cost several hundred euros a year, which is hard to justify out of pocket for a free tool. This is the one purchase users would actually feel.
  2. The blitcp.dev domain and its edge worker. The site itself is static and cheap; the domain and the worker behind the contact form and the update endpoint are not free.
  3. Hardware for the benchmarks. The numbers on the benchmarks page come from real drives — spinning disks, USB enclosures, SATA and NVMe SSDs — bought to be measured rather than borrowed from a cloud instance. Comparisons against cp, rsync and robocopy are only worth anything if the hardware underneath them is real.

Ways that help just as much

Money is the least useful thing most people can give an open-source project. These cost nothing and are worth more.

  1. Star the repository. It is the only signal that makes a tool findable by the next person with the same problem.
  2. File a good bug report. Most of what shipped in the last few releases — the silent abort on NFS destinations, quiet mode, the pip-aware updater — exists because somebody described what they saw. Say what you ran, what happened, and what you expected.
  3. Tell someone who moves a lot of data. This is a tool for people with a specific problem; it spreads by someone naming it at the right moment.

What donating does not change

Nothing about the tool. Every feature stays free, the licence stays Apache-2.0, and donors get no separate build. blitcp will keep printing nothing about donations during a normal run — not after a copy, not in --help, not on first launch — because a program that runs in cron and pipelines has no business advertising in them. The only two places it is mentioned are --version and the Settings panel of the GUI, both of which you opened on purpose.

There is no analytics on this page and no click tracking on the links above.
